Looks ready. Loads more.

Clueso is a team of AI agents that opens your site in a real browser before launch, checks what loads underneath, and shows you the proof for everything it finds.

Inspect a websiteSee how it works

Give it a website.

The agents will open your site in a fresh browser and:

  1. Record every requestmade before a visitor answers the cookie banner, and name the vendor behind it.
  2. Read your privacy policyand check each service it saw is named there, quoting the passage it matched.
  3. Measure store badgespainted size, ratio and clear space, against the official App Store and Google Play artwork.
Or try a public site:

Nothing runs until you press the button in the workspace. You review the evidence and decide what to change.

Four passes, one report.

Bar widths follow the work each agent finished in a real 53-second audit of a public site.

Inspect what actually happens.

Scout opens the page in Chromium with no cookies, records every network request before and after the consent banner, captures the policy text and finds every store badge on the page.

Fired before consent.

Every request the page makes before a visitor answers the banner is recorded with its time and its vendor: Google Analytics, Hotjar, Segment and the services the team has learned since.

Missing from the policy.

The privacy policy the page links to is read in full. Each service seen loading has to be named there, and the passage that names it is quoted beside the finding.

Stretched badges.

App Store and Google Play badges are measured as the browser paints them, then compared with the official artwork for ratio, minimum size and clear space.

Proof, or it stays a question.

A finding only counts once a second agent reproduces it in a fresh browser. What can't be settled is listed as a review item with what was observed and what is missing, never quietly dropped.

Sharper with every launch.

When the team can't inspect something, it proposes new knowledge or writes a checker, tests the frozen candidate on apps it has never seen, and keeps it only if it finds more without a single new false alarm.
